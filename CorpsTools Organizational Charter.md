# **CorpsTools Organizational Charter**

## **1\. Overview**

CorpsTools is a cadet-led organization dedicated to building, managing, and maintaining a collection of websites, applications, and digital tools tailored specifically for the Corps of Cadets. By leveraging the technical talents within the Corps, CorpsTools aims to create an easily accessible hub of resources that assist Cadets in their daily lives, ranging from academic and leadership tools to traveling and information management.

## **2\. Vision Statement**

To empower the Corps of Cadets through innovative, cadet-driven technological solutions that enhance daily operations, foster leadership, and streamline cadet life.

## **3\. Mission Statement**

CorpsTools serves as the centralized development and distribution hub for digital tools within the Corps. We bridge the gap between Cadet Clients and Cadet Developers by actively identifying operational challenges, developing robust software solutions, and seamlessly integrating them at the Corps-level. Through collaboration and continuous feedback, we provide a platform that promotes cadet ingenuity and solves real-world problems.

## **4\. Organizational Structure**

To ensure maximum efficiency and alignment with the needs of the Corps, CorpsTools is divided into two primary branches: the **Steering Committee** and the **Technical Team**.

### **4.1 The Steering Committee**

The outward-facing branch responsible for product management, user research, and client relations. They actively engage with the Corps to identify pain points, gather feedback on existing tools, and ideate new solutions.

### **4.2 The Technical Team**

The inward-facing branch responsible for the engineering, maintenance, and deployment of software applications. They turn the ideas and requirements generated by the Steering Committee into functional, secure, and user-friendly digital tools.

## **5\. Roles and Responsibilities**

*Note: Recognizing the demanding academic, military, and physical schedules of Cadets, CorpsTools operates on a flexible, volunteer-driven model. These roles provide necessary structure and direction, but are intentionally designed to be adaptable. Members contribute based on their current availability, and strict, rigid development cycles are avoided in favor of asynchronous, task-based progress.*

### **5.1 Command Leadership**

#### **Steering Commander (Overall Organization Lead)**

The Steering Commander guides the overarching strategy and vision of CorpsTools, focusing on advocacy and direction rather than micromanagement.

* **Guidance:** Serves as the primary leader and advocate for the organization, guiding the efforts of the Steering Committee.  
* **Strategic Direction:** Identifies long-term goals and ensures projects align with the actual, current needs of the Corps.  
* **Liaison:** Acts as the main point of contact for faculty and administration to pitch projects, secure resources, and ensure compliance.  
* **Feedback Integration:** Helps filter and prioritize informal feedback from the Corps into a flexible roadmap of potential projects.

#### **Executive Officer / XO (Second-in-Command & Technical Lead)**

The Executive Officer supports the Technical Team by removing roadblocks and maintaining foundational systems.

* **Technical Support:** Provides architectural guidance and mentorship for software development efforts.  
* **Operations:** Helps organize tasks into easily accessible project boards so volunteer developers can pick up work when they have free time.  
* **Bridge to Steering:** Works with the Steering Commander to turn ideas into easily understandable, bite-sized technical tasks.  
* **Continuity:** Maintains the core technical infrastructure (servers, databases, domains) to ensure projects survive cadet turnover and graduation.

### **5.2 Steering Committee Roles**

#### **Cadet Liaisons / Idea Champions**

* Act as the eyes and ears of the organization, noting daily challenges faced by the Corps.  
* Casually gather feedback from peers on existing tools and pitch new concepts.  
* Champion specific ideas, helping to explain the "why" and "how" to developers when they decide to pick up a project.

#### **Design & Testing Volunteers**

* Sketch basic wireframes or mockups to help visualize ideas before coding begins.  
* Act as casual beta-testers, providing a fresh set of eyes to click through and test new applications when they have free time.  
* Ensure tools are intuitive and genuinely helpful for the average Cadet.

### **5.3 Technical Team Roles**

#### **Project Maintainers**

* Cadets who adopt a specific application within the CorpsTools suite as their primary interest.  
* Help review code submitted by others and gently guide the general direction of their specific app.  
* Act as a knowledgeable resource and mentor for other developers working on that codebase.

#### **Volunteer Developers (Frontend, Backend, Mobile)**

* Build, test, and deploy code for CorpsTools applications on an asynchronous basis.  
* Pick up open tasks, bug fixes, or feature requests from a shared community board whenever their schedule permits.  
* Collaborate informally with Idea Champions and Maintainers to bring projects to life without the pressure of rigid sprint deadlines.

## **6\. Project Lifecycle**

1. **Ideation & Discovery:** The Steering Committee identifies a problem or receives a pitch from a Cadet Client.  
2. **Scoping:** The Steering Commander and XO review the idea for feasibility, impact, and resources.  
3. **Development:** Requirements are passed to the Technical Team. Software Engineers build the tool iteratively.  
4. **Testing & Feedback:** The Steering Committee tests the product, gathers beta-tester feedback, and logs bugs for the Technical Team to fix.  
5. **Deployment & Promotion:** The Technical Team deploys the application, and the Steering Committee promotes it to the Corps.
